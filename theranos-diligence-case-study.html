The Theranos Diligence Autopsy

A public-record reading of a diagnostics investment story

Semantic BIEpistemic RigorMarket Research
The lesson

The claims that carried the valuation were the ones an investor could have asked to see: which tests ran on whose machine, and who had really deployed it.

The case

Theranos is the best-documented example of a diagnostics story that outran its operating facts. At its height in 2014 the private company was valued at $9 billion, on the promise of a miniaturised blood-testing device that could run a wide range of tests from a few drops of blood. In 2015 a Wall Street Journal investigation questioned whether the technology worked as described. By March 2018 the US Securities and Exchange Commission had charged the company, its founder and its former president with raising more than $700 million from investors through exaggerated or false statements about its technology, business and financial performance.

The question the market asked: is this a revolutionary company? The question an evidence-graded briefing asks: which of these statements could a diligence team have checked in a week, and what would it have found?

How this case is used. Everything below comes from public records and press reports listed under Sources. AIMMS Consulting had no involvement with Theranos. This is a reading of the public record using the Semantic BI and Epistemic Rigor lenses, not a judgment on any individual investor’s diligence.

The first row carries the case. Whether a company’s own device runs the tests it sells is the single most checkable operating fact in diagnostics, and it was the one the offering leaned on hardest.

How the lenses read it

  • Semantic BI Analyst: the language was ambitious and unfalsifiable. “Revolutionary” and “a few drops” name no volume, no period and no comparator.
  • Epistemic Rigor Lens: a technology claim needs a validation method: independent comparison against established analyzers, published, with sample sizes. Demonstrations are not validation.
  • Mariana Trench Level Market Researcher: real laboratory economics start from tests billed, instruments owned and utilisation. None of these depends on the founder’s account.
  • Behavioral and Cognitive Bias Lens: halo effects from a charismatic founder and a prestigious board, and fear of missing out on a private round that others had already joined.
  • Pre-Mortem Foresighted Intelligence Analyst: assume the investment failed in year three and ask for the earliest sign. The first answer is a regulator or a journalist finding the gap between claim and operation.

Five requests that would have settled it

These are the requests an evidence-graded briefing would put to management in the first week. They are AIMMS’s reading of the case, not a statement about what any investor did or did not ask.

  • 1. Tests by instrument. A billed-test ledger showing which device ran each test.
  • 2. Independent validation. Comparison data from a laboratory the company does not control.
  • 3. Customer and partner confirmation. Direct contact with the named customers, not introductions through management.
  • 4. Regulatory standing. Inspection reports, accreditation scope and correspondence.
  • 5. Revenue to cash. Reported revenue reconciled to bank receipts and audited accounts.

Every one of these answers can be produced in days. A company that cannot produce them has told the panel something already.

Scope note. This case study reads public records and press reports. Theranos and its founder settled the SEC’s civil charges without admitting or denying them. Criminal convictions are as reported: Holmes was convicted on four counts and acquitted on four others. It is a commercial and evidential analysis, not legal advice.
