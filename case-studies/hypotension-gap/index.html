Bridging the Hypotension Gap

How field-level clinical vigilance on Concor 5mg and 10mg surfaced a dosing gap that internal management dismissed as unsolvable and how a physician-backed evidence pack turned it into a globally registered 2.5mg strength now carrying 65% of the brand's volume.

01 The Scenario

Post-Launch Vigilance

After the initial launch, responsibility for post-launch clinical monitoring in Lahore fell to AIMMS Consulting. Rather than treating launch as the finish line, a structured follow-up program was built in partnership with a panel of leading cardiologists Prof. Dr. Shaharyar Ahmed Shiekh (Head of Punjab Institute of Cardiology), Prof. Dr. Zubair Ahmed (Head of Cardiology, Mayo Hospital/KEMU), the late Prof. Dr. Mumtaz Hassan (VC KEMU, Principal KEMC), and Prof. Dr. Humayun Maqsood (Head of Cardiology, Sir Ganga Ram Hospital) to track real patient outcomes on the existing 5mg and 10mg doses.

02 The Problem

The Clinical Blind Spot

Three months of weekly follow-ups across 20-patient trial packages per unit surfaced a pattern that the existing dose range simply couldn't cover:

DoseObserved Outcome
10mgInduced hypotension (excessively low blood pressure) in most general patients.
5mgEffective as an anti-hypertensive, but still left a significant subset of patients feeling hypotensive and lethargic.
Physician workaroundProfessors were forced to recommend manually halving the 5mg tablet to approximate a safer dose.
Prof. Dr. Mumtaz Hassan

"We are satisfied with the efficacy, but for patient safety and precise dosing, we need a 2.5mg strength."

03 The Challenge

Overcoming Global Bureaucracy

Presenting these findings at the Business Sales Review met a fixed-mindset response from management: a new strength couldn't be launched in Pakistan unless it was first registered at the parent company's headquarters in Darmstadt, Germany. Other regional managers were skeptical it was worth pursuing.

The realization that shaped everything that followed: market demand alone wouldn't move a global parent company. It would only be heard if it arrived backed by clinical authority.

04 Strategic Intervention

Building the Endorsement Pack

1

Direct Advocacy

Worked with Prof. Dr. Mumtaz Hassan to draft a formal clinical recommendation letter for the 2.5mg dose.

2

Consensus Building

Visited each of the remaining panel professors individually to secure their official signatures and institutional stamps on the recommendation.

3

Global Escalation

Presented the completed clinical evidence pack to the MD, who forwarded it to Germany as a formal request for global product extension.

Key Issues & Strategic Solutions

Viewed through the AIMMS lens, this case is a study in the difference between managing a product and consulting on a market. The SKU that became 65% of the brand's volume was, for a period, invisible to the people managing the brand. Below is the issue-to-solution mapping AIMMS would apply to make that visibility structural rather than accidental.

Issue Product Management vs. Strategic Consulting

The internal default was to sell the SKUs already on the shelf and treat "halving a tablet" as an acceptable workaround, rather than asking what the market actually needed.

Solution Demand-Gap Diagnostics

A recurring post-launch review that explicitly asks "what workaround are prescribers inventing around our product?" turns a tolerated inconvenience into a flagged product-extension opportunity before a competitor spots it first.

Issue "Gold Plated Higher Management"

A fixed-mindset response at the Business Sales Review treated the German registration requirement as a wall rather than a process to be navigated with the right evidence.

Solution Evidence-Escalation Protocol

Field-level findings need a pre-built pathway to reach global decision-makers in a form they can act on a structured clinical evidence pack, not a verbal request, is what converts "market demand" into something headquarters can approve.

Issue Assumed Authority Over Field Evidence

Regional skepticism assumed the existing dose range was sufficient, without weighing what the prescriber community was already telling the field team.

Solution Mariana Trench-Level Market Research

Structured, sustained engagement with the prescriber community not a single survey, but three months of weekly follow-up is what surfaced a pattern invisible from head office. Depth of engagement is what separates real signal from anecdote.

Issue Clinical Authority Left Untapped

The consultant recognized early that market demand alone would not move a multinational parent company only clinical authority would.

Solution Institutional Consensus Building

Securing individual, signed endorsement from each member of a credible clinical panel rather than a single opinion builds a evidence pack that a global registration process cannot easily dismiss.

06 Key Lessons

For Management

Listen to the Prescriber Community

The doctors on the ground had already identified the exact gap and the exact solution months before it reached a boardroom.

Evidence Outranks Hierarchy

A signed, multi-institution clinical recommendation moved a global parent company faster than any internal argument could.

A Constraint Is an Opportunity in Disguise

What looked like a bureaucratic dead-end a required German registration became the pathway to the brand's single largest revenue driver.
