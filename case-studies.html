The Claim That Couldn't Survive the Descent

Situation

A regional pharma manufacturer had built a national launch campaign around three headline claims about efficacy and differentiation, due to go to media within weeks.

Descent

Semantic BI graded each claim; two came back Affect-Driven / Unfalsifiable. Pre-Mortem mapped what a competitor or journalist would say if challenged publicly. Brand Differentiation showed the underlying positioning was generic "innovation-led" language shared by half the category.

Surfaced with

A narrower claim set built on the one verifiable differentiator the product actually had — language specific enough to survive both legal review and public scrutiny.

Reading Market Depth Before Committing Capital

Situation

A commercial team was preparing to enter a new therapeutic category, with an internal business case built primarily on category-level growth figures.

Descent

A trench-level pass through IQVIA MAT data, prescriber concentration by tier, and channel margin structure showed the category's growth was concentrated in a single sub-indication most competitors were already crowding.

Surfaced with

A recommendation to enter through a narrower, less-contested indication first — building volume and prescriber trust before a full-category push.

Entry depth-4,000 m — rollout plan
Surfaced atAbyssal zone

Catching the Failure Signal Early

Situation

A diagnostics operator had a fully costed plan to open lab collection points across several cities within a single quarter.

Descent

Pre-Mortem ran the plan forward to its most likely failure point — new sites opening faster than referral partnerships and trained staff could be secured, leading to underused capacity in month two or three.

Surfaced with

A phased, tier-based rollout in which referral pipelines and staffing were locked in before each site opened rather than after — avoiding the exact failure mode the pre-mortem had surfaced.
