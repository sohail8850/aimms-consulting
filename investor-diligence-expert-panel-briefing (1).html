Expert Panel Briefing

Investor Diligence · Diagnostics Acquisition
Market ResearchSemantic BI
Outcome

A diligence memo the panel could act on without needing to re-verify every underlying claim themselves.

The brief

An investor group is evaluating the acquisition of a diagnostics business. The target’s information memorandum, management presentation and website describe a modern, high-growth, technology-led laboratory. Some of that is operating fact. Much of it is promotion. The panel has a few days, limited access to management, and no time to audit every sentence.

The assignment was to stand between the target’s materials and the panel’s decision: grade every material claim for evidence, mark what is verified, and hand the panel a memo where the remaining uncertainty is named, sized and priced rather than hidden inside confident language.

The real-world benchmark: Theranos

Diagnostics is the sector where the gap between claimed and actual capability has been most expensive. In March 2018 the US Securities and Exchange Commission charged Theranos, its founder and its former president with raising more than $700 million from investors through exaggerated or false statements about the company’s technology, business and financial performance. According to the SEC’s complaint, the company’s own analyzer could complete only a small number of tests, and the vast majority of patient tests ran on commercial analyzers made by others. Theranos and its founder settled without admitting or denying the allegations.

How this reference is used. The facts above come from public SEC records listed under Sources. AIMMS Consulting had no involvement with Theranos. The case shows what a diligence process exists to catch: the most important claims in a diagnostics deck are the ones about what the platform actually does and how much of the revenue it actually produces.

The evidence-grading scale

Every claim in the target’s materials was graded on the Semantic BI scale, adapted for investor use.

GradeMeaningTreatment in the memo
VerifiedTied to a source the panel can check: audited accounts, accreditation certificates, LIS or billing exports, signed contracts.Relied on, with source and date cited.
Plausible-UnverifiedReasonable and consistent with the market, but supported only by management assertion.Carried as a stated assumption, with a named test and an owner.
Affect-Driven-UnfalsifiableNo metric, period or comparator. Nothing could prove it wrong.Given no weight in valuation, and listed so nobody prices it in by accident.

What was tested

The claims below are illustrative of the types found in diagnostics acquisition materials. They are not figures from any client.

Typical claimGradeThe question that decides it
“Leading, technology-driven lab network”Affect-DrivenLeading by what measure: tests, revenue, accredited sites? Compared with whom, in which year?
“Fast-growing, 30% year-on-year”Plausible-UnverifiedRevenue or test volume? Organic or acquired? Does the base year include one-off or COVID-era work?
“Over [N] tests available”Plausible-UnverifiedHow many run in-house, how many go to reference labs, and how many were billed in the last 12 months?
“Proprietary, patented platform”Affect-DrivenWhat share of billed tests runs on it, versus third-party analyzers? Is the patent granted, and for which claim?
“Accredited, quality-assured laboratories”Verified if certificates matchWhich sites hold which accreditation, with what scope and expiry? Is proficiency-testing data available?
Audited revenue and monthly test volume per siteVerifiedReconcile billing-system exports to the accounts. This becomes the spine of the memo.

Key aspects of the diligence

01

Revenue quality

Split by test, site, channel and payer. Separate recurring clinician and corporate referrals from one-off or project work.

02

Concentration risk

Dependence on a few referring doctors, hospitals or corporate clients, and on a few collection points.

03

Technology reality

Instruments owned, utilisation per analyzer, share of tests run in-house, and the evidence behind any proprietary claim.

04

Quality and compliance

Accreditation scope, proficiency-testing record, turnaround times, repeat-test and error rates, regulatory correspondence.

05

Supply chain

Reagent and consumable dependence, import and currency exposure, supplier contract terms and lock-in.

06

Cash and working capital

Receivable days by payer type, collection history, capex needed to hold capacity, and normalised EBITDA.

07

People and governance

Key-person dependence on founders and pathologists, retention terms, related-party dealings.

08

Market position

Real catchment, competitor pricing and prescriber behaviour, read at trench depth rather than headline share.

How the engine was applied

  • Semantic BI Analyst: graded every claim in the target’s materials.
  • Epistemic Rigor Lens: tested how each growth, volume and margin figure was produced, including sample, period and definition.
  • Mariana Trench Market Researcher: rebuilt market size and competitive position from ground-level data instead of the deck’s top-down figures.
  • Behavioral & Cognitive Bias Lens: flagged halo effects, anchoring on the asking price and sunk-cost momentum within the deal team.
  • Pre-Mortem Analyst: assumed the deal had failed in year two and listed the first signals that would have shown it.
  • Strategic Management Advisor: reconciled the findings into a go, conditional-go or no-go view for the panel’s own decision.

The memo the panel received

  1. Verdict page. The conclusion in plain terms, with the three findings that most affect price or structure.
  2. Claim ledger. Every material claim, its grade, its source, and the test that would change the grade.
  3. Verified operating base. The revenue, volume and margin the panel can rely on today.
  4. Set-aside list. Promotional statements given no weight in valuation.
  5. Conditions and protections. Warranties, price adjustments, conditions precedent and earn-out hooks tied to the unverified items.
  6. Pre-mortem signals. The early warnings to monitor in the first two quarters after closing.
  7. Questions for management. A short, ranked list, so limited meeting time goes to the items that move value.

The panel could see which claims carried the deal and which were decoration. Re-verification was limited to the few items that remained Plausible-Unverified, each with a named test.

Scope note. This project is a representative engagement archetype: the sector and problem are real, and client identities are held confidential. It is an evidence and commercial review, not investment, accounting or legal advice. Valuation and transaction terms remain the responsibility of the investors and their qualified advisers.
