What the data shows

Progress is uneven across the three billions

In 2024 the world reached 1,377 million people on HPOP, 734 million on HEP and 513 million on UHC. HPOP passed 1 billion in 2022. On this series, HEP and UHC are still below the line in 2030, at 905 million and 892 million.

HEP dipped in 2021 before recovering, while UHC gains only picked up after 2021.

South-East Asia supplies about half of the gains

South-East Asia accounts for 56% of HEP, 52% of HPOP and 42% of UHC in 2024. The Americas is the one region with a negative HEP contribution (-25 million).

A handful of countries carry the totals

The five largest countries make up 62% of HEP, 79% of HPOP and 57% of UHC. India leads all three, with 309, 622 and 154 million people. Because these are absolute counts, population size drives much of this ranking; a per-capita view is the natural next step.

Some tracers pull backward

The largest gains come from the FUEL tracer (535 million), HPOP sanitation (458 million) and the HEP tracer ESPAR (424 million). Adult obesity moves the other way (-155 million in 2024) and worsens by 2030, as do child obesity and alcohol. Three tracers (child violence, development on track and intimate partner violence) are zero in every year, which more likely means missing data than no effect.

How the data was prepared

The raw WHO export was cleaned with a reusable function that logs every change it makes. The same function works on the original export or a previously cleaned copy.

StepResult
Structure15 raw columns reduced to 9 readable ones (year, geo_level, geo_name, billion, tracer, value, lower, upper, geo_code). Six single-value metadata columns removed.
Quality checksNo missing values, no duplicate rows, and unique keys on year, geography, billion and tracer.
ConsistencyCountry totals and WHO-region totals both add up to the global rows, so analyses must filter by geo_level to avoid triple-counting.
Shape of the values28.6% of values are zero and 25.8% are negative, so distributions use a signed log scale and outliers use a modified z-score.
Compact datasetThe earlier project file collapsed 35 tracers into 3 indicators, so 86,229 of its 94,068 rows shared the same year, region and indicator. The rebuilt triple_billion_dataset.csv has 7,839 rows with unique keys.

What to keep in mind

  • Each billion's value is the sum of its tracer contributions. WHO's published headline figures may use extra adjustments, so check them before quoting numbers.
  • Years after 2024 are treated as forward-looking. Confirm this against WHO's metadata.
  • Values are absolute counts, so large countries dominate. Adding population data would allow per-capita comparisons.
